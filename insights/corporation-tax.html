Corporation Tax is not always simply 25%

How the 19% small profits rate, 25% main rate and Marginal Relief fit together.

Updated 2 September 2026 • UK

It is common to hear that UK Corporation Tax is “25%”. For many companies that statement is incomplete. The rate depends on the level of taxable profits and other factors that can affect the thresholds.

The headline rates for 2026

HMRC’s published rates for Corporation Tax years starting 1 April 2026 show a 19% small profits rate for companies with profits under £50,000 and a 25% main rate for companies with profits over £250,000.

Between £50,000 and £250,000: qualifying companies can fall within Marginal Relief, which creates a gradual increase in the effective Corporation Tax rate rather than an immediate jump from 19% to 25%.

Why the thresholds are not always as simple as they look

The £50,000 and £250,000 limits can be affected by matters such as associated companies and the length of the accounting period. This means two businesses with the same accounting profit can have different Corporation Tax outcomes.

What Marginal Relief does

Marginal Relief reduces the amount payable at the main rate for companies whose taxable profits sit between the lower and upper limits. The purpose is to create a gradual transition from the small profits rate to the main rate.

The practical point

Business owners should avoid estimating Corporation Tax by automatically multiplying every profit figure by 25%. The correct calculation depends on the company’s taxable profits and circumstances.

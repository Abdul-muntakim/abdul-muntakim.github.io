IR35: what the off-payroll working rules are trying to do

A simple introduction to who the rules can affect and why employment status matters.

Updated 2 September 2026 • UK

IR35 is the common name used for the UK’s off-payroll working rules. The basic idea is that a contractor should not receive a significantly different tax outcome simply because they provide services through an intermediary, such as a personal service company, when the working relationship would otherwise look like employment.

When can the rules matter?

The rules can be relevant where an individual supplies services to a client through their own intermediary. HMRC says the key question is whether the worker would have been an employee for tax purposes if they had provided the services directly to the client.

Important: IR35 is about the reality of the working relationship, not just what the contract is called.

Who decides the status?

Responsibility depends on the type and size of the client. In many public-sector and medium or large private-sector engagements, the client is responsible for determining employment status for tax. Where services are supplied to a small private-sector client, the worker’s intermediary can remain responsible for deciding whether the rules apply.

What happens if the rules apply?

If a worker is treated as employed for tax purposes under the off-payroll rules, the deemed employer generally has responsibilities for deducting Income Tax and employee National Insurance from the relevant fees and paying employer National Insurance where applicable.

Why businesses and contractors should care

IR35 can affect contract pricing, cash flow, payroll responsibilities and the amount a contractor ultimately receives. It is therefore a topic worth considering before an engagement begins rather than only after a tax question arises.
